# 06 — Optional pelvic-floor practice

Pelvic-floor exercises may help some urinary or sexual symptoms, but they are
not required for gym progress and “more” is not always better. The app keeps
them optional and does not diagnose or promise a result. Persistent pain,
urgency, difficulty relaxing or sexual/urinary symptoms should be discussed
with a clinician or pelvic-floor physiotherapist.

## Find the muscle once

You may briefly identify the muscle used to stop urine, but do not repeatedly
interrupt urination as training. Another cue is the gentle lift used to prevent
passing gas. If you cannot isolate it, ask a qualified clinician.

## Gentle technique

1. Start lying or sitting comfortably.
2. Gently lift/squeeze only the pelvic floor; keep abs, glutes and thighs soft.
3. Hold for about 2–3 seconds, then relax fully for the same time.
4. Breathe normally. Stop before fatigue, pain or tightness.

Start with one short set on a few days per week. Only add another set if you
remain comfortable; there is no need to train three times every day. Never use a
pelvic-floor squeeze to brace a heavy lift, and never hold your breath.

The app's timer is a pacing aid, not a treatment. It can be skipped from the
workout without affecting the strength-session target.
