# 06 — Kegels for Men (Complete Protocol)

Sources: Mayo Clinic, Cleveland Clinic, Harvard Health. Kegels strengthen the pelvic floor —
the muscles supporting bladder/bowel and controlling sexual function.

## Benefits (evidence-backed)
- Better erectile quality and ejaculatory control
- Improved urinary control (no dribbling, better emptying)
- Stronger core "floor" → complements heavy lifting bracing
- Results typically noticeable after **6–8 weeks** of daily practice

## Step 1 — Find the right muscles (do this ONCE)
- Next time you urinate, stop the flow midstream. Those are the pelvic floor muscles.
  **Do not** practice this repeatedly (incomplete bladder emptying risk) — it's an identification test only.
- Alternative: imagine preventing passing gas, or "lifting" the penis/scrotum inward.

## Step 2 — The technique
1. Empty bladder first. Position: lying down (easiest) → sitting → standing as you progress.
2. Squeeze ONLY the pelvic floor. **Abs, glutes, thighs stay completely relaxed.**
3. Hold 3–5 seconds → fully relax 3–5 seconds (relaxation is half the exercise).
4. **Breathe normally throughout** — never hold breath.

## The Program (synced with your gym phases — Thursday + daily at home)

| Phase | Sets/day | Reps | Hold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Month 1 | 3 | 10 | 3 s hold / 3 s relax |
| Month 2 | 3 | 10 | 5 s hold / 5 s relax |
| Month 3 | 3 | 12 | 8 s hold / 8 s relax |

Spread sets across the day (morning/afternoon/evening). The app has a guided hold/relax timer
and logs each session. Nobody can tell you're doing them — do a set anywhere.

## Mistakes
- Squeezing abs/butt/thighs instead (put a hand on your belly — it shouldn't tense)
- Holding breath
- Overdoing it: more is NOT better; an over-tight pelvic floor causes its own problems.
  If you feel pelvic pain or urgency, take 2–3 days off and reduce volume.
- Expecting instant results — 6–8 weeks minimum, log it and trust the process.

## When to see a doctor
Pain during/after, or no improvement after 8 weeks of consistent practice → pelvic floor
physiotherapist (biofeedback exists and works).
