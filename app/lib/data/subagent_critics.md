# Review notes and decisions

**Reviewed:** 20 September 2026

The earlier critic file contained unsupported locked assumptions (age, body size,
medical status, a claimed 500-food database, a six-day target and mandatory
kegels). Those claims are not used by the app now.

## Strength and hypertrophy

- Keep three full-body sessions because they are easier for a first block and
  practise each movement more than once weekly.
- Start with 1–2 work sets and build toward 2–3 only when technique and recovery
  are stable.
- Use controlled reps and about 2–3 reps in reserve by default. No max testing
  and no failure sets for a beginner's squat, press or hinge.
- Add small direct arm work only if it improves enjoyment or a later goal; it is
  not required for the first weeks.

## Stamina

- Optional walk/cycle sessions at a conversational pace are a sustainable
  starting point. Build duration gradually instead of imposing HIIT.
- Farmer walks are useful but are not mandatory; the card provides a safe
  suitcase-carry or easy-walk alternative.

## Core, posture and mobility

- Dead bugs, planks, rows, face pulls and a pain-free mobility circuit provide
  simple practice. No exercise “fixes” posture by itself.
- A movement that causes pain is substituted or stopped; the app does not claim
  one machine is universally safer.

## Food

- The offline catalog contains a modest set of transparent approximate entries,
  not 500 verified foods. Prices vary by brand, season and Delhi market.
- No fixed calorie or protein prescription is shown until the user supplies
  relevant goals and health context. A regular meal pattern and a protein
  source are enough to begin.

## Pelvic floor

- Kegels are optional, gentle and separate from lifting technique. Do not train
  by repeatedly stopping urine. Pain, urgency or persistent symptoms require a
  qualified clinician or pelvic-floor physiotherapist.
